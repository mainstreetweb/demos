01 The practice Est. 2004 — Madison, WI

A roof is the only part of a house that is asked to fail. Ours are built to argue with that.

Ray Vandenberg started Northgate in 2004 with one truck, a compressor, and a habit of walking the roof twice before quoting it. His daughter Erin runs the office now; his nephew Tomás runs the crews. Same rule as day one — no shortcut ever saves more than it costs.

Wisconsin is hard on roofing. Freeze-thaw cycles from November to April, ice dams at every eave, and a hail season that can undo a decade in nine minutes. We build for that specifically: full ice-and-water membrane at the eaves and valleys, ring-shank nails, balanced intake and exhaust ventilation, and decking replaced wherever the sheathing has gone soft.

11 Questions Answered plainly

Asked &
answered.

Q.01How do I know whether I need a repair or a whole roof?

Age, layers, and the state of the decking. A twelve-year-old roof with one bad valley is a repair. A twenty-five-year-old roof with curling tabs, granule loss in the gutters and soft spots underfoot is a replacement, and patching it is money spent twice.

We tell you which one you have before we tell you what it costs, and we put the photos in the estimate so you can see what we saw.

Q.02Are you licensed, bonded and insured?

Yes — Wisconsin Dwelling Contractor and Qualifier credentials, general liability, and full workers’ compensation on every person who steps on your property. Certificates of insurance come to you directly from our carrier, which is the only version worth accepting from any contractor.

Q.03How long does a typical replacement take?

Most Madison houses are three to five working days: tear-off and dry-in on day one, install through days two and three, gutters, flashing details and clean-up at the end. Complex roofs — many valleys, dormers, or a metal system — run longer, and we say so before you schedule, not after.

Q.04What happens to my landscaping and my lawn?

Beds and shrubs get covered, the dumpster sits on plywood, and the lawn and driveway are swept with a rolling magnet twice — once at the end of tear-off day and once on the final day. If you find a nail afterwards, we come back and sweep again. That has happened four times in twenty-two years, and we still came.

Q.05Do you handle insurance claims after a storm?

We document the damage properly — measured, photographed, and written up in the format adjusters expect — and we meet your adjuster on the roof so the conversation happens between two people who are actually looking at it.

We do not negotiate on your behalf or advise you on your policy; that is your insurer’s job and yours. We just make sure nothing real gets missed.

Q.06Is any job too small?

No. A cracked boot, a lifted ridge cap, one length of gutter. Small work is how most of our long-term customers first found us, and the truck that comes out is stocked for it.

Q.07Can you work in winter?

Repairs and emergency work, always — a leak in January does not wait for April. Full replacements need the right conditions for the shingles to seal properly, so we schedule those around the weather and tell you honestly when a job should wait for a warmer week.

Q.08Do you use subcontracted crews?

Never. Every roofer on your house is a Northgate employee, on our payroll and under our insurance, trained by our own leads. It is the single biggest reason our workmanship warranty is worth the paper it is written on.
