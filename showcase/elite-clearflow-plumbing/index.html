What's wrong?
Tap it, and we'll tell you what we'd do.

No guesswork and no scare tactics. Pick the symptom you're seeing and you'll get the honest version: the likely cause, what we'd check first, and how urgent it really is.

A tap that won't stop dripping

Usually a perished washer, a scored ceramic cartridge, or a seat that's been chewed up by Charlotte's hard water. A single dripping tap can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year — and the noise is the least of it.

  1. 1

    Identify the valve type — compression, ceramic disc or cartridge mixer — because the fix is completely different for each.

  2. 2

    Isolate at the local service valve rather than the whole house, so the rest of the property keeps running.

  3. 3

    Strip and inspect the seat. If it's pitted we re-cut it, because a new washer on a bad seat drips again within weeks.

  4. 4

    Replace washers or cartridge, re-grease, reassemble, then run and check under load for 10 minutes before we pack up.

Urgency: book it in Typically a single visit. If the same tap has been repaired twice, we'll tell you honestly that replacing it is the better call.

Water standing in the sink, bath or yard

Slow but clearing usually means grease and soap build-up close to the trap. Backing up across several fixtures at once means the blockage is further out — often roots in the lateral, or a bellied section of pipe holding water.

  1. 1

    Check which fixtures are affected. One slow sink is local; multiple fixtures point at the main line.

  2. 2

    Camera survey down the cleanout so you can watch the screen with us and see exactly what's in there.

  3. 3

    Clear it with the right tool — rodding for soft blockages, high-pressure jetting for grease and scale, a cutting head for roots.

  4. 4

    Re-survey after clearing. If the pipe itself is damaged, you get the footage and a written repair option, not a sales pitch.

Urgency: within a day or two If sewage is backing up into the property, treat it as an emergency and call us straight away.

No hot water, or it runs cold too quickly

On a tank system it's usually a failed heating element, a tripped thermostat or a tank full of sediment. On a tankless unit it's more often scale in the heat exchanger or a flow sensor that's stopped reading properly.

  1. 1

    Confirm the fuel and unit type, check for lockout codes, and verify the gas or electrical supply is actually reaching the heater.

  2. 2

    Test thermostats and elements, or on tankless units read the fault history and inlet/outlet temperature spread.

  3. 3

    Flush the tank or descale the exchanger. In hard-water Charlotte this alone brings a lot of "dead" heaters back to life.

  4. 4

    Check the expansion tank and T&P relief valve while we're there — these fail quietly and matter for safety.

Urgency: same or next day If you smell gas or see water pooling under the tank, shut off the supply and call the emergency line.

A burst pipe or active flooding

Every minute counts, so the first thing we do is get the water stopped — over the phone if we're still on the road. Then we make safe, dry out, and only then talk about the permanent repair.

  1. 1

    Call us and we'll walk you to your main shut-off valve while the van is moving. Turn off the water heater supply too.

  2. 2

    On arrival we isolate the failed section, cap it, and get pressure back to the rest of the house where we safely can.

  3. 3

    Photograph everything for your insurer, note the cause, and give you a written make-safe record the same day.

  4. 4

    Permanent repair or section replacement, plus a check of nearby pipework — bursts rarely happen to healthy pipe.

Questions

The things people ask
before they call.

If yours isn't here, the phone is answered by an engineer and the advice is free.

Do you really answer the phone at night?
Yes. There's an engineer on the overnight rota every night of the year, including holidays. You get a person who can talk you through shutting the water off, not a message service that logs a callback for the morning.
How quickly can you get to an emergency?
Our average arrival time across Charlotte is 41 minutes. Outer areas such as Huntersville and Mint Hill can take a little longer at peak times, and we'll tell you a realistic number on the phone rather than an optimistic one.
Will I know the cost before you start?
Always. We diagnose first, then give you a written, itemised quote with options where options exist. Work only begins once you've agreed it, and the figure doesn't change afterwards unless you ask us to do something extra.
Can you fix it in one visit?
Most repairs, yes — the vans carry common parts for the fittings used in Charlotte housing stock. Larger jobs such as repipes or heater replacements are booked in properly so we can protect the house and work through it without rushing.
Do you clean up afterwards?
Floor runners and dust sheets go down before any tool comes out, and everything is vacuumed at the end of each day. On multi-day jobs we leave the house usable every evening — including the water back on wherever it's safe.
What does the guarantee actually cover?
Five years on our workmanship, printed on your invoice. Parts are covered by whatever the manufacturer offers, and because we're an approved installer for the brands we fit, those warranties stay valid. Care plan members get seven years while the membership is active.
Are you licensed and insured?
Licensed for plumbing in North Carolina and carrying 2 million dollars of general liability cover plus workers' compensation. The certificate goes out with the quote automatically — you shouldn't have to ask for it. (Registration numbers shown on this concept site are fictional.)
Do you work with insurance claims?
Regularly. For any escape-of-water incident we photograph the cause and the damage, write up a make-safe record, and send it the same day so your claim isn't held up waiting on a report from us.
